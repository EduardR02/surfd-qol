Surfd QoL
Source on GitHub

See where your speed goes.

Surfd QoL is a Chrome extension for surfd.net. See where you lose speed on each ramp and in the air, review the moment in a replay, then try the section yourself.

Choose the feedback you want, with a configurable HUD, replay tools and local run history.

Download for Chrome Free and open source (MIT).
Desktop Chrome 118 or newer.

Install

It isn't on the Chrome Web Store, so you load it into Chrome yourself. It works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and Opera.

Surfd QoL needs a Chromium browser: Chrome, Edge, Brave or Opera. Firefox isn't supported yet.

  1. Download the zip and unzip it.Keep the unzipped folder somewhere it can stay; your browser loads the extension from it.
  2. Open chrome://extensionsPaste it into the address bar, then switch on Developer mode in the top right.
  3. Click Load unpacked and pick the unzipped folder.It's the folder that contains manifest.json.
  4. Open surfd.net.The tab reloads once while the extension hooks in. Settings are under QoL, bottom right of the game menu.

Chrome will show a “started debugging this browser” bar. The extension needs the debugger permission to read the game's physics, and only attaches to surfd.net. You can close the bar; everything keeps working until you reload.

Updating

A link above Runs and QoL in the game menu lets you know when a new version is available. Chrome won't install it automatically.

  1. Download the latest zip and unzip it.Replace the contents of the extension folder you already loaded.
  2. Reload the extension, then refresh Surfd.Open chrome://extensions and click the reload icon on Surfd QoL. Your settings and saved runs stay in this browser.

Private rooms

Surf with friends in a room of your own: no time limit, no map votes, and the host picks the map. Everyone in the room needs Surfd QoL.

Host a room
Open Find a server and click Host a room. Pick a map, a name, a player limit, and Private (code or link) or Public (listed under Community). Hosting needs a surfd.net account, and your browser runs the room, so keep the tab open.
Join
Pick a room in Find a server's Community tab, type its code next to the filters, or open an invite link.
In the room
Chat, spectating, map changes and finished runs work like on Surfd's servers, and savelocs are shared: everyone's saves show in the list, up to 20 each per map. Runs count for the leaderboard as in solo play, checked by Surfd.

Replays and run history

Surfd’s replay player gets savelocs, Runs and a mark on the timeline for each touch, in the color of its grade. Prefer Surfd QoL’s own bar? Switch to it in settings. Both work on your recorded runs, leaderboard replays and the KSF record route, with the touches marked on every one.

Cinematic replays
Switch any replay from POV to a Steady or Cinematic camera planned around the route. Add your PB, your recent best, the WR or any leaderboard run as ghosts with their trails, racing in real time or side by side, each with its gap to the run you’re watching.
Practice from a replay
Save a position with Saveloc or your usual save bind, then leave the replay and load it in practice to try the section yourself.
Local run history
Open Runs for your latest attempt, recent runs, best recorded finish and recent best. In Ramps, compare your best, last 10 measurements or average with your PB or the WR. Each ramp shows how often you reach it and the measurement you pick (Surf, Touch, Angle, Down, Up, speed in or out) against the reference; click it for the touch angle (how far your landing was from parallel to the ramp; 0° loses no speed), your speed in and out, and replays to watch it in. Recording and storage settings are under QoL → History.

What the numbers mean

Under the speed, a bar of fixed width splits your last flight and the ramp you landed on into their parts, in the order they happened, each in the color of its grade. A tick marks where the ramp would have ended at the reference's cost; past it is what you lost. Each value stays up until the next ramp or flight replaces it, so you can read it in the air instead of mid-ramp. All of them are in units per second. Turn the bar off to keep just the values.

−2.6touch
−34.4down
−16.2up
Touch
Speed clipped off as you land on a ramp. Height conversion doesn't count.
Down
What the ramp cost from the touch to the lowest point you reached, the touch included.
Up
What it cost from the lowest point to where you left the ramp. Down and Up add up to the whole ramp, touch included.
Air
Your last flight: speed lost to braking plus gain missed by imperfect strafes. It's the left part of the bar.
Surf
The whole ramp, touch included. While you surf, the bar shows just Air and this, the ramp's loss so far.
  • As good as the reference or better
  • A little worse
  • Worse
  • No matching reference

You choose what to compare against: the KSF record route (the default), Surfd's leaderboard #1, or your own PB. Even the record loses real speed on most ramps, so the comparison tells you more than the raw number.

Settings

  • Turn any value on or off; the rest re-centre.
  • A compact HUD while you surf (no timer unless you want one) and the full one in replays, or the full one everywhere.
  • Zen mode for just speed and brief start stats, without hiding the replay’s hull indicator.
  • Move, resize and recolor the HUD with a live preview.
  • Grade each value against fixed targets, your PB, the KSF record or Surfd's #1.
  • Optional crouch-jump on any key: one tick of crouch per jump.
  • Optional nullbinds for left/right and forward/jump, following your game's movement bindings.
  • A strafe preview: from the start zone, watch your view turn the way a good strafe would at any speed, on the ground or in the air, with the key to hold.
  • Speed counter and timer refresh rates, so digits stay readable.

The game's physics and timing stay unchanged. Settings and recorded runs stay in your browser. Hidden feedback skips its analysis.
